# 🤝 Capgemini Behavioral & PowerSkills Assessment (SJT) Guide

The **PowerSkills / Behavioral Assessment** is a situational judgment test (SJT) designed to evaluate whether your working style, team dynamics, ethics, and temperament match **Capgemini's 7 Core Values**.

---

## 🌟 The 7 Core Values of Capgemini (The Scoring Rubric)

Every question in the PowerSkills assessment maps directly to one or more of these 7 values (established since 1967 by founder Serge Kampf):

1. **Honesty:** 
   - Refusal of unfair or unethical business practices. Transparent communication. Admitting mistakes immediately rather than hiding them.
2. **Boldness:** 
   - A willingness to take calculated risks, embrace innovation, show initiative, and step outside your comfort zone.
3. **Trust:** 
   - Confidence in your team members, delegating work, respecting agreements, and earning the trust of clients and colleagues.
4. **Freedom:** 
   - Independence in thought and action, creativity, entrepreneurial spirit, respecting diverse perspectives.
5. **Fun:** 
   - Feeling good about being part of a team, celebrating achievements together, maintaining high morale and positive energy.
6. **Modesty:** 
   - Humility, listening attentively to others, continuous learning, avoiding arrogance even after major wins.
7. **Team Spirit:** 
   - "Together we are stronger". Prioritizing collective team success over individual glory. Supporting a struggling colleague.

## 🎯 How Situational Questions are Evaluated

### Scenario Archetype 1: Conflicting Deadlines or Overwork
- **Scenario:** *"You are assigned a critical task due tomorrow, but your senior asks you to review another client document by this evening. What do you do?"*
  - ❌ **Bad:** Silently try to do both and end up missing both deadlines (dishonest about capacity).
  - ❌ **Bad:** Flatly refuse the senior rudely (damages team spirit).
  - ✅ **Best (Capgemini Way):** Proactively communicate immediately. Inform the senior of your existing deadline, estimate the time required for both, and collaboratively agree on priority or seek delegation.

### Scenario Archetype 2: Disagreement in a Team Meeting
- **Scenario:** *"A teammate proposes a technical architecture approach that you believe has serious scalability flaws. What is your response?"*
  - ❌ **Bad:** Stay quiet to avoid conflict (lacks Boldness & Honesty).
  - ❌ **Bad:** Publicly criticize the teammate harshly (lacks Modesty & Team Spirit).
  - ✅ **Best (Capgemini Way):** Acknowledge the positive aspects of their idea first, then share your concerns with objective data/examples, inviting open discussion.

### Scenario Archetype 3: Finding a Bug Right Before Release
- **Scenario:** *"One hour before a major client deployment, you discover an edge-case bug. Fixing it may delay the release."*
  - ❌ **Bad:** Deploy anyway and hope nobody notices (Violates Honesty & Trust).
  - ✅ **Best (Capgemini Way):** Flag the bug to the project lead immediately with an assessment of the impact, risk, and a proposed quick workaround.

## ⚠️ The Consistency Trap
- The test system asks the **same underlying scenario in 2 or 3 different wordings** across the 25 minutes.
- If you answer one way on Question 4 (e.g. prioritizing risk-taking) and the opposite way on Question 22 (e.g. extreme risk avoidance), your **"Reliability / Consistency" score drops**.
- Always respond from the perspective of an **honest, collaborative, proactive team player**.
